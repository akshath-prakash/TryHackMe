The CIA Triad

Overview

The CIA Triad is a foundational information security model made up of three principles: confidentiality, integrity, and availability. These principles help security professionals understand what must be protected and how security controls should be designed.

The Three Principles

Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that digital information is only available to authorized individuals.

Examples of confidentiality controls include:

Usernames and passwords

Access permissions

Encryption

Multi-factor authentication

Integrity

Integrity ensures that digital information is not modified, deleted, or manipulated without authorization.

Examples of integrity controls include:

File permissions

Hashing

Digital signatures

Change monitoring

Availability

Availability ensures that information and systems are accessible when authorized users need them.

Examples of availability controls include:

Backups

Redundant systems

Disaster recovery plans

Protection against denial-of-service attacks

Important Findings

The CIA Triad provides a framework for identifying security goals:

Principle

Security Goal

Confidentiality

Prevent unauthorized access to information

Integrity

Prevent unauthorized changes to information

Availability

Keep information and systems accessible when needed

What Do We Protect in Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity protects information, systems, networks, devices, applications, and services from unauthorized access, modification, destruction, or disruption.

Different security incidents can affect different parts of the CIA Triad. For example, a data leak affects confidentiality, a manipulated file affects integrity, and a service outage affects availability.

Conclusion

The CIA Triad is one of the most important foundations of cybersecurity. Confidentiality protects information from unauthorized access, integrity protects it from unauthorized changes, and availability ensures that it remains accessible when needed.
